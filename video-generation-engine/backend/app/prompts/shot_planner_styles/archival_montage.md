## Style override: archival_montage

This project uses the `archival_montage` style - documentary footage cut noticeably harder than the default, with full-frame text cards used deliberately and often as chapter markers. The following overrides the base instructions above where they conflict; everything else (one idea per shot, intent, historical grounding of the `prompt` field) still applies.

- **Cut harder than the default, not as frantic as a reel.** You will be given tighter duration bounds than usual (roughly 1-4 seconds per shot, target around 2). Prefer shots near the middle of that range; use the full length only when one idea genuinely fills it. Do not manufacture extra shots to fill time - cover each fragment range with the fewest shots that respect the bounds.
- **Transitions: `cut` is the default; `dissolve` where eras or moods shift.** The montage reads through rhythm, so most transitions are plain cuts. Keep `dissolve` for a genuine change of place, time, or mood inside the scene, and reserve `wipeleft`/`fadeblack` for the scene's biggest structural beat, exactly as the base prompt says.
- **Camera: restrained moves, executed briskly.** `pan` and `slow_push` carry most shots - at this cut pace even slow moves read as energetic - with `punch_in` reserved for `emphasize` beats and `pull_back` for reveals. `static` only when the narration genuinely lingers.
- **Use `text_card` more than you otherwise would - but still purposefully.** This style leans on full-frame text cards as chapter markers and fact callouts: give roughly one card every four to six shots, on shots whose content states a date, a turning point, or a number worth reading (e.g. "1923: Hyperinflation", "12 million out of work"). A card's text must be short (a handful of words), self-contained, and never narration or dialogue. Every other shot keeps `text_card` as the empty string.
- **Respect the shot-count and duration limits you are given exactly** - they are tuned specifically for this style's pace, not the general-purpose defaults.
